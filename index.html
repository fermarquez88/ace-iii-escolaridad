Education-adjusted ACE-III calculator

Expected ACE-III score by years of education and age. The current rule compares everyone against one of two numbers. This tool compares each person against what is expected for their education and age.

Illustrative values. They come from 342 community participants in San Juan who meet the study's control criterion: word-list recognition score ≥ 10, no history of stroke or head trauma, and independence in basic activities of daily living. Not population norms: a methodological demonstration based on those 342 controls. The control group may include people with undetected mild impairment, which would make the 5th percentile a lenient threshold. Do not use these values to make decisions about a patient.

How it was estimated

Regression of the total score on years of education, its square, age, and sex, with a second model for the dispersion — log(residual²) ~ education + age — that lets variability change with education. The dispersion includes Harvey's correction, without which it would be underestimated 1.9-fold. Calibration: within the sample, 5.8% of controls fall below the nominal 5th percentile of the model shown here; under cross-validation, 5.6% fall below it with the beta-binomial model used as a robustness check.

Who it describes

The sample comprises 342 community participants from San Juan who meet four conditions — word-list recognition score ≥ 10, no stroke, no head trauma, and independence in basic activities of daily living — a criterion independent of the ACE-III and with no educational gradient (χ² p = 0.504). The values are not population norms but a methodological demonstration based on these controls. The sample may include undetected mild impairment, which would make the 5th percentile a lenient threshold. Most controls are between 50 and 80 years old; data are sparse below 3 years of education.

What it does not do

It neither diagnoses nor replaces clinical assessment. A score below the 5th percentile indicates that such performance is uncommon among unimpaired people of the same education and age; nothing more.

Origin of the two-step rule

The two-step rule used in practice (86 from 12 years of education, 68 below) combines cutoffs from different sources. The 86 comes from the Argentine–Chilean validation of the ACE-III, which proposed a single cutoff in a sample averaging more than 13 years of education; the 68 comes from another study. Neither source proposed the combined rule.
